TL;DR

  • The menus in your screenshots hold about 55 effects and 210 settings. Only about a third of them matter for voice and podcast editing. The rest are music tools (Paulstretch, Vocoder), lab tools (DTMF tones, Chirp) or editing plumbing (Invert, DC offset).
  • "AI learns the settings" isn't model training. We give the AI a structured catalogue: every effect, every parameter with its range and unit, a plain-English description of what it does to a voice, plus about 50 worked prompt → setting examples. The model then picks values through tool calls. This works with today's models and needs no training data.
  • The AI also needs to measure the audio first (loudness, pitch, how bright or muddy, noise, echo), so "make it warmer" adapts to this recording instead of applying the same preset every time.
  • Don't show users 80 sliders. Show 6 character sliders (Thin↔Full, Dark↔Bright, Close↔Roomy and so on) that each move several real settings, with an "Advanced" section that shows the exact effects underneath. This is the invideo-style panel, adapted for audio.
  • About 90% of it can be built with what we already ship (ffmpeg). The one gap is quality pitch and formant shifting, which voice changes like thinner/deeper need. That needs Spotify's free pedalboard library (built on Rubber Band). A few things only AI models can do (remove echo, change who you sound like) and need ElevenLabs or similar.

How the AI uses these settings

Understand"Make my voice a little thinner" becomes a goal: less low-end weight, slightly higher, more forward.
MeasureAnalyse this recording: pitch, low-end energy, brightness, loudness, noise.
ChoosePick effects from the registry and set values that suit those measurements.
PreviewRender a 10–20 s preview; EQ and volume preview instantly in the browser.
Show panelCharacter sliders plus the exact settings used, all adjustable.
Save"Done" saves a new version. The settings stay editable and can be undone.

The important design choice: effects are stored as data, not baked in. Each version keeps an ordered list like [{effect: "high_pass", freq_hz: 140}, {effect: "low_shelf", freq_hz: 250, gain_db: -4}, …]. Turning one off, reordering or nudging a slider re-renders from the original, so quality never degrades from repeated edits. This is the same idea as Audacity's "realtime effects" stack.

Recommended v1 "effect registry" for the AI

22 effects and about 80 parameters. That covers roughly 90% of what podcasters and voiceover creators ask for, can be built with ffmpeg plus pedalboard plus the ElevenLabs isolator we already use, and is small enough for the model to use reliably.

Clean

Voice isolation (Strength, Keep background) · Noise reduction (Amount, Sensitivity) · Noise gate (Threshold, Reduction, Attack, Hold, Release) · Hum removal (50/60 Hz, Harmonics) · Click removal (Sensitivity) · De-esser (Amount, Freq) · Clip repair (Threshold) · Breath reduction (Amount)

Tone

High-pass (Freq, Slope) · Low-pass (Freq, Slope) · Low shelf (Freq, Gain) · High shelf (Freq, Gain) · 5-band parametric EQ (Freq, Gain, Q × 5) · Presence boost (Gain)

Voice character

Pitch (semitones + cents) · Formant shift (−3…+3) · Speed / tempo (0.5–2×, keep pitch)

Dynamics & level

Compressor (Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release, Knee, Make-up) · Auto-level (Target, Max boost) · Loudness target (LUFS preset per platform) · Limiter (Ceiling, Release)

Space

Reverb (Room size, Damping, Pre-delay, Wet, Dry, Width) · Echo/Delay (Time, Feedback, Mix)

Timing & mix

Silence trimming (Max pause, Threshold) · Fades (In, Out, Curve) · Music ducking (Amount, Attack, Release) · Bleep (word, tone Hz) · Insert pause (Duration)

Each registry entry holds: id, label, plain-English purpose, perceptual notes (e.g. "high-pass 120–180 Hz removes chest resonance, so the voice sounds thinner, younger, more 'phone-like' above 300 Hz"), parameters (type, min, max, default, unit, step, safe range), whether it previews live in the browser, and render cost. One definition automatically drives the AI tool, the slider UI, the REST route and the MCP tool.

Every slider change is loudness-matched (output normalised to the same LUFS), so "brighter" never just means "louder". That's the classic trap that makes A/B comparisons dishonest.

Gap: high-quality pitch and formant shifting

Neither our bundled ffmpeg nor the Homebrew one includes rubberband. Without it, a pitch shift either changes speed too (asetrate, chipmunk) or sounds rough. Fix: add Spotify's pedalboard Python package (free, open source). It ships Rubber Band built in, plus good Reverb, Compressor, Chorus, Distortion, Delay and Limiter. One pip install, no system dependency, works on Railway.

Instant preview in the browser

The Web Audio API can run EQ, shelves, filters, gain, compression and convolution reverb live while you drag a slider. That covers the Tone, Dynamics and Space groups. Pitch, formant, noise and AI effects need a short server render (about 1–3 s for a 15 s preview), triggered when you let go of the slider.

Cost and speed

All DSP effects run on our server, free per use. Only AI effects cost money: ElevenLabs isolation (already used), voice changer, and the OpenAI call that turns a prompt into a recipe (a fraction of a cent).

Safety rails

Every parameter has a "safe range" the AI must stay inside. Output is always loudness-matched and limited, so a bad AI guess can't blast someone's ears or clip. Every apply is a new version, so it's always undoable.
